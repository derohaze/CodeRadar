Pointing a reviewer at an unfamiliar repository is a consequential action. The codebase is too important to be scanned without clarity about scope, depth, and limits.

CodeRadar treats onboarding a repo as a controlled workflow, not a casual button press.

## Before the first finding

- Choose file or folder scope deliberately.
- Choose fast or deep review for the time budget.
- Choose a preset: safe, balanced, or aggressive.
- Confirm provider settings — or run detectors-only.
- Know that truncation will be reported, not hidden.

## The CodeRadar approach

CodeRadar's onboarding direction is built around control, visibility, and accountability. Teams should be able to review at scale while still understanding what the review covered and what it could not verify.

Discovery indexes the repo, mapping traces its structure, and segmentation narrows the queue — before any model reads a line. A review that reports nothing then means one of two explicit things: clean code, or a stated limitation. Never a mystery.

## The CodeRadar outcome

Onboard once, trust the stream: scope first, model second, evidence always.
